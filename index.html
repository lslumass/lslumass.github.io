Research

Phase behavior of biomolecules and polymers across scales

How molecules self-assemble, phase separate, and aggregate underlies both cellular function and the design of new materials. My research develops physics-based computational models that bridge molecular detail and mesoscale behavior, and uses them, together with experiments, to understand and predict these processes in living and synthetic systems.

01

Physics-based multiscale models

All-atom simulations are accurate but slow, while simple coarse-grained models miss the molecular features that matter for biology. I develop intermediate-resolution models that close this gap, for proteins (HyRes), nucleic acids (iCon), and small molecules, and integrate them into one framework for simulating complex biomolecular mixtures at near-cellular scale. The models are calibrated against experiments and all-atom simulations, combined with machine learning, and shared as open-source tools.

Key work: JACS 2024 · PNAS 2025 · bioRxiv 2026

02

Biomolecular condensates

Biomolecular condensates organize the cell through phase separation, and their dysregulation is linked to diseases from neurodegeneration to cancer. I study how the sequence, structure, and interactions of proteins, nucleic acids, and small molecules determine how condensates form, what they contain, how they behave as materials, and how they age into pathological aggregates, with the long-term goal of guiding condensate-targeted therapeutics.

Key work: JACS 2024 · Biochem Soc Trans 2024 · PNAS 2025

03

Amyloid fibrils and their fuzzy coats

Amyloid fibrils in neurodegenerative diseases have an ordered core surrounded by a disordered "fuzzy coat" that has long been hard to see in experiments. Using large-scale simulations, I study how fuzzy coats are organized and how they shape fibril stability, surface properties, and interactions with chaperones, membranes, and small molecules.

Related work: JACS Au 2026

04

Integrating simulation and experiment

Close collaboration with experimental labs runs through all of my work. Simulations provide molecular-level explanations of experimental observations and testable predictions, across biomolecular recognition, membrane interactions, and condensation in cells.

Related work: JACS Au 2026 · JACS Au 2026 · Biophys J 2026 · bioRxiv 2026 · Biomacromolecules 2024

05

Polymer self-assembly and materials design

The same physics governs synthetic materials. I use simulation to understand how polymer sequence and architecture control self-assembly and phase behavior, and to design new nanostructures for smart materials, working with experimental groups to test the predictions.

Models

Open models you can run

All models are open source, with setup tools and tutorials on GitHub.

Proteins

HyRes

Hybrid-resolution protein model that captures secondary structure and its coupling to phase separation, with GPU-accelerated simulation of dynamic proteins in complex environments.

JACS 2024 HyRes preprint 2026 Tutorial HyresBuilder
RNA

iConRNA

Intermediate-resolution RNA model with explicit Mg²⁺ that reveals the driving forces of RNA condensation, including base pairing and stacking.

PNAS 2025 GitHub
DNA

iConDNA

Intermediate-resolution model of dynamic DNA structures and their phase separation, compatible with HyRes and iConRNA.

bioRxiv 2026
Unified framework

HyRes/iCon

Proteins, nucleic acids, ions, and small molecules in one consistent intermediate-resolution framework, for simulating multicomponent condensates at near-cellular complexity.

bioRxiv 2026 Setup tools

Other tools: iConMapper (machine-learning atomistic-to-coarse-grained mapping) · SciKit (analysis and plotting toolkit) · all repositories →

Database

Open data for disordered proteins

Large-scale simulation ensembles and curated experimental data, shared openly for analysis, benchmarking, and machine learning.

Simulation ensembles

HyRes-IDRome

HyRes conformational ensembles for 29,872 disordered proteins and regions from the human proteome and DisProt, with trajectories, radius of gyration, end-to-end distance, secondary structure, and sequence properties for each. Also available as predicted entries in the Protein Ensemble Database (PED 2027).

Hugging Face dataset Browse in PED
Amyloid fibrils

Fuzzy Coat Atlas

HyRes-sampled fuzzy coats across known amyloid fibril structures, with surface properties, internal interactions, and binding properties of the disordered regions that flank the fibril core.
